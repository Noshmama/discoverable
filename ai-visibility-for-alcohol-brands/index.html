The good news is you can fix the visibility problem without touching the compliance one.

Why regulated brands get skipped

The brands an assistant names are the ones easiest for it to read and corroborate. Regulated brands lose on a specific set of things:

  • A hard age gate blocks the crawlers. A full-screen “Are you 21?” wall that hides the page until a click can stop the readers that feed AI answers from seeing anything. These hard gates aren’t required by the TTB or any law; they come from a voluntary code, and a lighter version keeps you compliant and readable.
  • The facts are trapped in PDFs and images. Specs, ingredients, and details live in downloads a machine can barely read. If you can’t highlight it with your cursor, the AI can’t either.
  • Over-hedged copy says nothing a machine can quote. Legally-scrubbed pages often state so little that there’s nothing concrete for an assistant to lift. You can be factual and clear without making a claim.
  • The name isn’t consistent, and there’s no outside corroboration. Across a three-tier world your name fractures, and an assistant checks independent sources before it vouches for you.
