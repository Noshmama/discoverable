How Do I Make My Business Discoverable on AI?

More buyers now ask an assistant instead of a search bar. Here’s the plain-English answer to how you become the brand it names — and the steps to get there.

The short answer: To make your business discoverable on AI, make your website easy for a machine to read and easy for it to trust. That means putting your key facts on the page as real text, going by one consistent name everywhere you appear, adding a bit of behind-the-scenes code that tells machines what your page is, letting the AI crawlers in, and earning mentions from sources the AI already trusts. An assistant can’t taste, touch, or try your product — it recommends the brands it can most clearly read and verify. Becoming one of them is a set of fixable choices, not an ad budget.

That’s the whole thing in a paragraph. Here’s what each part actually means.

1. Put your facts in plain text

The single most common mistake: burying your important details in images, PDFs, or a video. They look great to a human and are nearly invisible to a machine. Your product details, ingredients or specs, your story, your prices — put them on the page as text you can highlight with your cursor. If you can’t select it, an AI can barely read it.

2. Answer the real questions, in writing

Assistants pull from pages that plainly answer the specific things buyers ask: what’s in it, where it’s from, how to buy, how the subscription or club works, whether you ship to their state. A brand story written like poetry gives a machine nothing to quote. A clear FAQ and honest, factual pages give it clean sentences it can lift straight into an answer.

3. Use one name everywhere

Your name on your own site, on the retailers and directories that carry you, on Google, on the review sites in your category — they all need to match, exactly. When three slightly different versions of your name float around the web, you teach the machine you’re three faint strangers instead of one brand worth trusting.

4. Add the “hidden labels” (structured data)

There’s a small chunk of behind-the-scenes code you can add to a page that never shows on screen — its only job is to tell a machine “this is the brand,” “this is the product,” “this is a 4.3-star review.” It’s like the tag sewn into a shirt collar: the shopper never sees it, but it spells out exactly what everything is. Most sites skip it, which leaves the AI guessing. You won’t touch it yourself — just ask whoever runs your site to add structured data for your brand, products, and reviews.

5. Make sure you’re not locked out

Some sites accidentally block the very crawlers that feed AI answers — often a heavy drag-and-drop build, or a full-screen gate that hides everything until someone clicks. If the machines can’t get in, you can never be recommended. It’s worth a quick check.

6. Earn the mentions AI already trusts

AI leans on sources it knows: press coverage, a real presence on the marketplaces and review sites in your category, genuine reviews. If nobody credible mentions you, the machine has no reason to either. This is the slowest of the six — and the most durable.

The test that tells you where you stand

Open ChatGPT and type “recommend a [your category] like [a competitor].” Are you named? Try Gemini and Google’s AI Overviews too. If you’re not there, one of the six things above is why — and every one of them is fixable.

None of this rewards the biggest budget. It rewards the brand that’s easiest for a machine to read and verify. I proved it on my own tool, COLAClear: with zero ad spend, Google’s AI Overview started citing it by name as the tool for its job. The same moves work whether you sell bourbon, skincare, or software.

Questions people ask

How do I get my brand recommended by ChatGPT?

Make your site easy for ChatGPT to read and trust: put your facts in plain text, answer buyers’ real questions in writing, use one consistent name everywhere, add structured data, allow AI crawlers, and earn credible mentions. ChatGPT recommends brands it can clearly read and verify, not the ones that spend the most.

Why doesn’t ChatGPT mention my company?

Usually because the machine can’t read or verify you. Common causes: your key details are trapped in images or PDFs, your name is inconsistent across the web, your site blocks AI crawlers, or no trusted source mentions you. Fix those and you become eligible to be named.

How do I show up in Google’s AI Overviews?

The same foundations that earn a page-one ranking — clear, question-shaped content, structured data, and authority — make you eligible to be quoted in AI Overviews. There’s no ad you can buy for it; Google’s AI cites the sources it can most clearly understand and trust.

Do I need to pay to get found by AI?

No. Getting recommended by AI is about being machine-readable, not about ad spend. COLAClear earned a by-name citation in Google’s AI Overview with zero paid media — through clear text, one consistent name, structured data, and open crawler access.

How is getting found by AI different from SEO?

SEO earns a ranked link on a results page; getting found by AI (GEO) earns a mention inside the assistant’s answer. They share the same foundations, but GEO adds machine-facing signals — like structured data, an llms.txt brief, and open AI-crawler access — aimed at being quoted, not just linked.
