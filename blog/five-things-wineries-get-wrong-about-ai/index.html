Five Things Wineries Think Help Them Get Found by AI (That Don’t)

You’ve been told you need to “get found by AI,” so you did a few things that feel like progress. Here are five of the most common — why they don’t move the needle, and what actually does.

1. Cramming keywords into everything

The instinct: repeat “best Napa Cabernet” enough times and the machine will notice. But AI doesn’t match keywords, it matches meaning — and stuffing the same phrase everywhere is a classic spam signal that can work against you. What works instead: write the way a buyer actually asks — by occasion, pairing, and budget — in plain language.

2. Calling yourself “award-winning” on your own site

Superlatives on your own pages are nearly worthless to a machine, for the same reason they’re worthless to a skeptical shopper: it’s you praising yourself. AI checks what independent sources say about you. What works instead: real reviews and complete listings on Vivino, Wine-Searcher, and CellarTracker, plus genuine press. (More on that here.)

3. A gorgeous PDF tasting sheet

Your tech sheet looks beautiful, and a machine can barely read it. Facts locked inside a PDF or baked into an image might as well not exist. What works instead: put the words on the page as real, selectable text an assistant can actually lift.

4. Being active on social media

You post constantly, so you must be discoverable — right? Social platforms are mostly walled off from the crawlers AI uses, and a caption isn’t the machine-readable, on-site fact an assistant needs to recommend you. Social builds an audience; it doesn’t make you readable to AI. What works instead: your own site’s text, plus the third-party databases AI trusts.

5. Treating it as a one-time fix

You paid someone for “SEO” three years ago, so you figure you’re set. Two problems. That work aimed at Google’s blue links, not at whether an assistant can read and recommend you — a different job. And discoverability isn’t set-and-forget; reputation and content compound over time. What works instead: keep your facts current, keep earning third-party mentions, keep answering the new questions buyers ask.

None of these five are foolish. They feel like progress, which is exactly why they’re so easy to mistake for the work. The real work is less glamorous: be readable, be corroborated, be specific, and keep at it. Do that, and you become the wine the machine names.
