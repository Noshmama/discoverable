The Winery Website Checklist for Getting Found by AI

More buyers are asking an assistant — “recommend a small-production Sonoma Chardonnay,” “which Paso wineries sell allocations to the public?” — and the AI names a handful. Here’s how to make sure yours is on that list.

Whether your winery gets named has surprisingly little to do with what’s in the glass. An assistant builds its answer from what it can read about you online — your site, your listings, your press, your reviews. So the wineries that get recommended are the ones easiest for a machine to understand. (For the fuller story on why that is, see Why AI Recommends Some Wineries by Name.) Here’s the checklist I’d run on any winery site, with the jargon defined as we go.

1. Get your words out of pictures and PDFs

Tasting notes, tech sheets, your whole story — wineries love to post these as beautiful images or downloadable PDFs. A machine can barely read either. Quick test: try to highlight your Cabernet’s tasting note with your cursor. If you can’t, neither can the AI. Put the words on the page as real text.

2. Answer the questions buyers actually ask — in writing

The appellation, the blend, whether you’re organic or dry-farmed, how allocations work, whether people can visit, how the club works. Plain, factual answers give the machine clean sentences it can quote. Poetic copy gives it nothing to work with.

3. Go by one name, everywhere

Your winery name, your label brands, your appellation, your address — they need to match across your own site, Vivino, Wine-Searcher, CellarTracker, Google, and your distributors’ listings. When “Smith Family Vineyards,” “Smith Family Winery,” and “Smith Cellars” all float around, you teach the machine you’re three faint strangers instead of one trusted name.

4. Add the “hidden labels”

There’s a small chunk of code you can add to a page that never shows on screen — its only job is to tell a machine what each thing is: “this is a winery,” “this is a wine,” “this is a 4.3-star review.” It’s called structured data, and it’s like the tag sewn into a shirt collar — the visitor never sees it, but it spells everything out. Most winery sites skip it. You won’t touch it yourself; just ask whoever runs your site to “add structured data for the winery, the wines, and reviews.”

5. Make sure you’re not locked out

Search engines and AI send out automated readers (called crawlers) to read your pages. A full-screen “Are you 21?” wall — or a heavy drag-and-drop site build — can accidentally block those readers, so they see nothing. You usually don’t need to drop the age check; you need it built so the page still loads underneath for the crawlers. If they can’t get in, you can never be recommended, so it’s worth having someone check.

6. Earn the mentions AI already trusts

Coverage in the wine press, a real presence on Vivino and Wine-Searcher, genuine reviews. These are the sources an assistant leans on to decide you’re real and worth naming. It’s the slowest of the six — and the most durable.

The test

Open ChatGPT and type “recommend a winery like [a peer of yours] in [your region].” Are you named? Try Google’s AI answer and Perplexity too. If you’re not there, one of the six above is why — and every one is fixable.

One honest note on timing

None of this is instant. After you make the changes, search engines have to re-read your pages and rebuild their picture of you, which takes weeks. This is a season’s work, not an afternoon’s — but it compounds, and it costs no ad budget.
