How to Tell If AI Can’t See Your Brand

When a buyer asks ChatGPT to recommend something in your category, does your brand come up? A seven-point self-check you can run in ten minutes.

More buyers are skipping the search bar and just asking an assistant: “recommend a good [your category].” The AI names a few brands and ignores the rest — and it has less to do with which one is best than with which one the machine can actually read. Here’s how to tell if yours is invisible.

1. Are your words real text, or trapped in pictures?

Try to highlight a product description on your site with your cursor. If you can’t — it’s baked into an image or a PDF — a machine can barely read it. Put it on the page as text.

2. Can the machine even get in?

Some sites accidentally block the automated readers that feed AI answers — a heavy drag-and-drop build, or (for beverage brands) a full-screen age gate that hides everything until you click. Open your site in a private window: if it loads nothing but a wall, that’s a problem.

3. Do you answer the questions buyers actually ask?

Not the brand story — the specifics: what’s in it, where it’s from, how to buy, how the club or subscription works. Plain written answers give the machine something clean to quote. Poetic copy gives it nothing.

4. Do you go by one name everywhere?

Your name on your own site, on the retailers and directories that carry you, on Google — do they all match? Three spellings teach the machine you’re three faint strangers instead of one brand worth trusting.

5. Do your pages carry the “hidden labels”?

Every web page has two layers: the part you see — words, photos, buttons — and the code underneath that builds it. Some of that code never shows on screen; it’s there only for machines to read. Structured data is a small chunk of that invisible code you add to a page. It changes nothing a visitor sees — its only job is to spell out what each thing on the page is: “this is the brand,” “this is the product,” “this is a 4.3-star review.” Think of the little tag sewn into a shirt collar: the shopper never notices it, but it says “100% cotton, size medium, made in Portugal.” Structured data is that tag for your web page — hidden from the customer, read by the machine, telling it exactly what everything is so it doesn’t have to guess. Most sites skip it. You won’t touch it yourself: just tell whoever runs your site to “add structured data for your brand, products, and reviews” — a quick, routine job — then confirm it with Google’s free Rich Results Test.

6. Have you earned the mentions AI trusts?

AI leans on sources it already knows: press, a real presence on the review sites and marketplaces in your category, genuine reviews. If nobody credible mentions you, the machine has no reason to.

7. The real test — just ask

Open ChatGPT and type “recommend a [your category] like [a competitor].” Are you named? Try Gemini and Google’s AI Overviews too. If you’re not there, checks 1–6 are why.

None of this is about having the best product. It’s about whether the machine can read you well enough to recommend you — and the brands that get named aren’t the best on the shelf, they’re the easiest for AI to understand. The good news: every one of these is fixable, and none of it takes a big budget.
